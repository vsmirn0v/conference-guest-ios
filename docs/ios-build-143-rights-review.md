# Rock’n’Roll iOS build 143 distribution review

Reviewed 20 November 2026 against the signed 0.2.0 (143) archive built from `3a314abf9f2144d6de76167caa73fc891979ec3a`. The app owner confirmed authorization for the native adapters on 20 November 2026. The reviewed binary still needs updated third-party notices and consent for remote AI. Replacing a proprietary SDK changes the binary evidence. It does not by itself grant a third-party service license.

## What ships

The app and its broadcast extension depend on the Rock’n’Roll meeting engine and customized LiveKit Swift/WebRTC implementation. The signed app links `LiveKitWebRTC.framework` and `RustLiveKitFFI.framework`. LiveKit's Swift code and nanopb, MLX Audio Swift and its dependencies are also included. The archive did not contain a Jazz, Telemost or TrueConf provider SDK framework. Its only standalone license resources were the older WebRTC compilation and the MLX Audio Swift MIT license. The linked website described browser dependencies and parts of the iOS stack. It did not provide the full iOS notices or corresponding UniFFI source location when checked.

The independent iOS artwork is documented in `docs/icon-design.md`. There is no commercial music, stock video or third-party provider logo in the iOS app icon. This review does not establish clearance for unexamined screenshot subject matter or a trademark registration.

## Rights to access meeting services

A meeting invitation can demonstrate the host's intent to admit a guest. Who controls each endpoint and what its operator permits also matter. Apple's 5.2.2 requires that accessing a third-party service be specifically permitted under its terms, with evidence upon request. Where a customer operates a LiveKit deployment and may authorize a compatible client, its own authorization and the documented token flow can provide that basis. A token issued by a hosted service is not blanket evidence that an independently implemented client or transcript extraction is allowed under the operator's terms. The app owner expressly confirmed authorization for the native adapters. Supporting contracts, territorial coverage and provider correspondence were not independently inspected. Keep the owner’s evidence available if Apple requests it.

- **Yandex Telemost.** The adapter uses an anonymous HTTPS bootstrap and signaling inferred from the public web client, recorded in `docs/telemost-native-integration.md`. The service terms §2.1 allow guest calls and §2.2 contemplates partner products. The published business REST API covers management, meetings, broadcasts and messages. Neither identifies the adapter's media signaling flow as a licensed public integration. The Yandex User Agreement §5.2.10 restricts commercial use of its services or access without permission or an express allowance. Yandex 360 general terms §4.4 restrict copying service information, including automated access, subject to written permission. Which content is service information and which party uses it are fact-specific. [Service terms](https://yandex.ru/legal/telemost_termsofuse/en) · [Yandex User Agreement](https://yandex.ru/legal/rules/) · [Yandex 360 general terms](https://yandex.ru/legal/360_termsofuse/) · [Published Telemost API](https://yandex.ru/dev/yandex-360/telemost/ru/)
- **TrueConf.** The adapter selects the server's advertised browser client and speaks its native guest signaling, as documented in `docs/trueconf-native-integration.md`. The public administrator API documents scoped third-party OAuth. The EULA §§8/8.1 limits reverse engineering and imposes conditions if a mandatory-law decompilation exception is used. §17.1 provides for third-party development using its tools and restricts competing/replacement functionality and circumvention of license metrics. An invitation or an operator's right to provision guest seats alone does not establish this particular native client's license. Interoperability using independently written code does not itself establish that decompilation occurred. [TrueConf EULA](https://trueconf.com/company/legal/terms.html) · [Server API authorization](https://trueconf.com/docs/server/en/admin/api/)
- **SberJazz / compatible enterprise guest links.** The paid service agreement §§1.8, 1.14, 1.20 and 2 describes a territorial nontransferable license and an SDK-based integration offering. The end-user agreement §4.1 limits usage to the offered functionality and §4.3 restricts technology inspection, decompilation, automated messages/calls and automated information collection. These provisions warrant specific clearance for the independent client, imported transcripts, and optional assistant automation. The SDK license is separate from the app's own native code; the use case and storefronts must fit the applicable operator contract. [Service agreement](https://developers.sber.ru/docs/ru/policies/jazz) · [User agreement](https://platformv.sbertech.ru/files/jazz/polzovatelskoe-soglashenie_Jazz.pdf)

Record the scope of the confirmed rights, including covered endpoints, data features and storefronts. If an integration falls outside that scope, the app could open its invitations in the provider’s official client/browser while retaining supported self-hosted rooms. Restricting store copy alone leaves the native access unchanged. Give Apple ordinary-user steps and testable access for every active integration, including those described generically in the public listing. [Apple §§2.5.19, 5.2.1–5.2.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

## Meeting data and AI

The build's iOS settings offer Apple on-device generation or a user-configured external endpoint. The default preference favors the Mac with local fallback. Automatic generation is enabled by default; selecting a remote service and key can change the destination of future summaries. The iOS provider controls do not state what meeting content is sent or solicit explicit permission to share it with that endpoint. The more detailed consent strings in the localization catalog were referenced by Mac-specific views. Distinguish consent to sending meeting data to a model from approving a proposed reply to participants. Apply a permission gate for remote transfers, including manual generation, automatic summaries and remote assistant calls, reconfirming changes of recipient. Check disclosure of notes, chats and transcript fields against the actual payloads. Clarify recording/voice consent where the app and host support those features.

The privacy page checked on 20 November said transcripts were excluded from sync. The shipping app includes optional raw-transcript iCloud retention, a local/archive workflow, and synchronized personal tasks. Reconcile those settings with the public privacy policy and the App Store privacy questionnaire, including data stored by configured providers. [Apple §§2.5.14 and 5.1.2(i)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) · [App privacy definitions](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/)
